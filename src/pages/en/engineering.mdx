  <div class="tw-balance">
    Agents write the code, I steer the process -- from the first specification to the final review. Behind it is a clearly structured workflow:

    <dl class="workflow-list">
      <dt>Spec-Driven Development</dt>
      <dd>Concepts and requirements take shape in dialogue with an agent and are precisely specified before the first line of code is written.</dd>
      <dt>Context Engineering</dt>
      <dd>Project rules, architecture decisions, and documented knowledge give agents the context they need&nbsp;&ndash; versioned in the repository and more solid with every project.</dd>
      <dt>Agent Skills</dt>
      <dd>Recurring workflows are encapsulated as reusable skills, for translation, operations, and maintenance, for example.</dd>
      <dt>Multi-Agent Orchestration</dt>
      <dd>Specialized agents work in parallel, connected via MCP and plugins&nbsp;&ndash; model-agnostic, whether Claude, Codex, or Gemini.</dd>
      <dt>Owning quality</dt>
      <dd>Automated tests, linting, and CI pipelines safeguard every step. My review has the final say.</dd>
    </dl>

    This is how this website and the administration of my own infrastructure come about. Experience makes the difference: anyone who has known semantics, accessibility, and UX for decades sees immediately whether an agent has done clean work.

  ## Semantics. For Humans. For Machines.

  <div>
    Semantic HTML is no relic. What makes content accessible to screen readers also makes it interpretable for AI models. Clean markup of structure and meaning has always been the foundation of good frontend engineering -- in the age of AI, it's more relevant than ever -- agents, too, work better with clear structure.

    For me, that's nothing new. Semantics has been part of my work from the start -- and that's exactly what makes the difference: while others retrofit it, I've brought this quality for 30 years.

  </div>
</section>

  <div>
